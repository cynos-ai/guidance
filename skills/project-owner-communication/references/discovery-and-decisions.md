# Discovery and decision dialogue

Use this mode to turn an incomplete outcome, hidden assumptions, or several viable paths into a decision the owner can understand and act on.

## Anchor on the observable flow

- Start with what the project must let a user, operator, creator, or connected system accomplish.
- Walk through the end-to-end scenario before discussing component or vendor choices.
- For a flow with at least three stages, draw one compact map and reuse it. A correction to the final result must propagate backward through affected stages and decisions.
- Distinguish a product outcome from an intermediate artifact. Do not quietly substitute “generate source material” for “deliver a finished video,” for example.

## Explore with Socratic questions

Use questions to expose a decision, not to perform a scripted interview. Select only the useful line of inquiry:

- **Clarify:** What does the outcome mean in observable terms? What would count as complete?
- **Surface assumptions:** What must be true for this route to work? Which assumption is being treated as fact?
- **Test evidence:** What supports the claim? What cheap observation or experiment could disprove it?
- **Consider alternatives:** What different responsibility split or route could produce the same outcome?
- **Trace implications:** What changes for users, operations, cost, safety, or future choices if this is adopted?
- **Question the frame:** Are we solving the right decision, or has an implementation detail replaced the real goal?

Start wide when the owner knows the domain and needs room to define the outcome. Narrow the question when ambiguity blocks progress or the option set is small. Once the answer is inferable, recommend it instead of continuing to ask.

## Converge on a decision

For a material choice, organize the reasoning as:

1. **Situation:** the outcome, current constraints, and why a decision is needed.
2. **Options:** a small set of credible paths, including deliberate inaction when it is genuinely viable.
3. **Criteria:** the few factors that could change the choice.
4. **Comparison:** trade-offs against those criteria, with uncertainty visible.
5. **Recommendation:** one preferred path, why it wins here, and what would overturn it.

Lead with the recommendation in conversation when the evidence is already strong. Use the full sequence when criteria or trade-offs still need agreement.

## Maintain decision state

Track:

- confirmed outcome and non-goals;
- chosen path and rationale;
- rejected paths or corrected assumptions;
- unresolved decisions;
- evidence or PoC needed next.

Show only the delta during normal turns. Provide the compact full state at a checkpoint, after a major correction, or when closing the session.

## Stop

Stop questioning when the owner can make the current decision and remaining uncertainty has a named validation step. Do not force agreement on implementation details that are reversible or owned by the delivery team.
