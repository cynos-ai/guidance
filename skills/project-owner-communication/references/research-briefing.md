# Research briefing

Use this mode when external technical facts, vendor or model capabilities, commercial terms, or rapidly changing information materially affect a project decision.

## Preserve the owner outcome

Restate the capability the project must provide and the non-goals before comparing mechanisms. Evaluate providers and models against that contract; never redefine the contract around what one supplier happens to offer.

Present capabilities and responsibility boundaries before names. Explain why different roles may need different model or vendor classes, then map current candidates to those roles.

## Use two information layers

### Owner view

Keep the first layer compact:

- what the project will be able to provide;
- the recommended route and responsibility split;
- why that combination fits;
- the main trade-off or material risk;
- what requires a PoC before commitment.

Use one stable end-to-end capability map when the route has multiple stages.

### Technical evidence

Put supporting detail after the owner view:

- official capability evidence and source date;
- vendor/model primary and fallback candidates;
- API, format, quality, latency, region, licensing, and operational constraints that affect the choice;
- prices with currency, unit, tier assumptions, and checked date;
- parameters, links, and internal integration notes when useful;
- a PoC plan for claims that documentation cannot establish.

Do not mix a long supplier inventory into the primary recommendation.

## Label the nature of claims

Use these distinctions where they change trust or action:

- **Verified:** supported by a current primary source or direct observation.
- **Assessment:** a reasoned recommendation or comparison based on the available evidence.
- **To validate:** dependent on actual project assets, quality thresholds, scale, or behavior that only a PoC can establish.

Do not decorate every sentence with a label. Apply them to material claims, comparison rows, and unresolved risks.

## Research discipline

- Prefer official documentation, pricing, model cards, release notes, and direct tests over summaries.
- Attach dates to volatile capability and price claims.
- Separate unavailable evidence from a negative finding.
- Compare candidates using criteria derived from the owner outcome, not a generic feature checklist.
- Recommend a default when evidence is sufficient. Ask the owner only about a trade-off that changes the product outcome, material cost, risk, or hard-to-reverse direction.

## Stop

Stop when the owner has a recommendation, its evidence boundary, and a bounded PoC for the remaining material unknowns. Do not continue collecting vendors after additional candidates are unlikely to change the decision.
