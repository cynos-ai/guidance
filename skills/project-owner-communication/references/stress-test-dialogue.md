# Stress-test dialogue

Use this mode when the owner explicitly wants rigorous challenge rather than a normal explanation or decision brief.

## Set the target

- State the plan, intended user-visible outcome, non-goals, and current confidence in compact form.
- Agree on what kind of failure matters: product failure, unsafe behavior, operational fragility, cost, schedule, adoption, lock-in, or another material consequence.
- Challenge the plan to improve the decision, not to display skepticism or prolong the interview.

## Choose the lightest effective challenge

- **Assumption probe:** identify the few beliefs whose failure would overturn the plan; ask what supports them and how to falsify them cheaply.
- **Scenario challenge:** walk a difficult but plausible user or operational path through the end-to-end model and find where the promised outcome breaks.
- **Premortem:** assume the initiative has failed, describe the observable failure, identify likely causes, then update goals, mitigations, and validation priorities.
- **Alternative frame:** construct the strongest credible competing route and compare it against the same outcome and criteria.

Do not run every method. Select the one most likely to expose a material blind spot.

## Control the interview

- Resolve dependency chains one branch at a time when an answer changes the next question.
- Group related independent questions when they can be answered together without losing clarity.
- Explain what decision each question tests and provide a recommended answer when evidence supports one.
- Accept a reversible default without demanding confirmation.
- Keep already rejected assumptions closed unless new evidence reopens them.
- Rank issues by impact and plausibility; do not spend equal time on remote edge cases.

## Update the decision model

After a material answer, update only what changed:

- strengthened or weakened assumption;
- changed outcome or boundary;
- new mitigation;
- rejected route;
- required experiment or evidence.

At the end, summarize the strongest surviving plan, the most important residual risks, and the next validation step. Separate a flaw in the plan from an unknown that needs evidence.

## Stop

Stop when further questioning is unlikely to change the decision, mitigation, or validation plan. Rigor means resolving material branches, not exhausting every imaginable question.
